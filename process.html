30 Sep to 3 Oct

Five docks in four days

Each version answers the main objection to the one before. The pictures show the same view: the leg root on one face, and that face alone.

  • Leg root (PLA)
  • Dock on the body (printed with it from V80 on)
  • Bought steel: latches, keepers, bearings
  • Spring bolt (printed)
  1. V7930 Sep

    Receiver and two draw latches

    A square foot drops into a separate receiver, which is screwed to the face with six M2 screws. Two draw latches clamp it down.

    Why it changedThe face itself is not the interface. The dock alone needs an adapter part and 14 screws per leg, and only four faces are prepared for it.

    ▶ Animation: the latch in 9 steps

  2. V802 Oct

    Triangular foot, rim printed on the face

    The foot becomes a triangle that slides under a V-shaped rim printed with the body. The receiver and its six screws are gone: screws per leg 36 → 30.

    Why it changedStill only four faces had a dock. Quick release should mean any face.

    ▶ Assembly bench of V80

  3. V813 Oct

    The same dock on all 20 faces

    Every face gets the rim. The two latches move to a beam in front of the foot and lie flat.

    Why it changedA re-check showed that the 45° roofs are not a hard stop: with both latches open the foot can slide 5.2 mm straight back out (unless the friction on the roofs is above about 0.4), so the two latches alone hold the leg. The body now carries 40 keepers and 80 screws, and the robot is up to 3.55 kg.

    ▶ Assembly bench of V81

  4. V823 Oct

    Proposal from GPT: add a steel cross pin

    Same diagnosis. Keep V81 and add a Ø5 steel pin (violet) through two ears on the dock and through the leg root, held by a cotter pin (pink) and two washers.

    Why it was not builtFour loose parts per leg and a pair of pliers. The pin passes three Ø5.5 holes, which asks for about ±0.13 mm on every printed face. Latches, keepers and screws all stay. +48 g.

  5. V833 Oct · current

    Two printed spring bolts, no metal in the dock

    The latches go. Two spring bolts in the leg root snap out in front of two solid walls that are printed with the body. Pinch both paddles to release.

    ResultLeg 0.667 → 0.598 kg, robot 3.55 → 3.15 kg (estimates). The only bought parts in the lock are two springs and two M2 screws per leg.

    ▶ Animation: how a leg clicks on

V83

How the leg is held

A drawer with a door bolt. The triangular foot slides in under two 45° roofs, so the only way out is to slide 5.2 mm straight back the way it came. Two spring bolts close that path. The Quick release tab shows it step by step.

5.2 mmthe leg must slide back this far to come off, with both bolts held in
7.0 mmbolt extension when locked, as designed (7.7 as it really rests). A seat 0.5 mm off either way gives 5.1 to 8.9 mm
μ ≥ 0.134friction the wedge needs to hold by itself. Printed plastic: about 0.2 to 0.4 (assumed, not measured)

Tolerance window and how to read it on the part

As designed, the lock works for a seat that is up to 0.7 mm short or 0.7 mm deep, which is about ±0.35 mm on the printed surfaces. With the bolt where it really rests under load (found on 6 Oct, below) the window is 0.95 mm short to 0.51 mm deep, and the bolt stands 7.7 mm out instead of 7.0.

It can be read without tools: the paddle shows as much as the bolt is out. 5 to 9 mm is right. Less means the face is fat (trim the contact lands under the roofs, or the pads); a paddle at its travel stop that still rattles means it is thin (tape on the pads).

Print estimates

Leg root 10 h 16 m, body 38 h 36 m, cap 12 h 05 m, tilted test face 6 h 13 m (PrusaSlicer estimates). Bambu Studio estimated the first plate at 5 h 48 m where PrusaSlicer gave 16.5 h, so expect roughly a third of these times on the H2D.

3 to 5 Oct

Three outside looks at V83

Each reviewer read only the delivered files. What they found, and what changed because of it.

3 Oct · on the first draft

Re-check from the STEP files only

A second AI session received the exported STEP files and no source code, and re-measured the key dimensions with its own code: 10 of 10 confirmed. 7 remarks, 4 of them adopted:

  • Side gap between the guide bosses and the ends of the stop walls 0.7 → 0.3 mm. With one bolt gone the far side now swings back 0.7 to 0.9 mm instead of 1.6 to 1.8 (hand statics), and the missing bolt can snap out again if its side has not moved back more than 1.1 mm (in a loose fit, push the leg forward by hand first).
  • Bolt clearance in its channel 0.15 and 0.10 → 0.25 mm.
  • R0.4 at the step of the stop wall, which is on the tension side.
  • A test print of one face in the attitude it has on the body (T2): on four faces one rail prints horizontally, and its groove needs support.

Not taken: guards around the paddles, spare screw holes.

4 Oct · read-only

Review 1 by GPT

Its verdict: the lock works geometrically and the small test pieces can be made, but nothing yet shows that it stays tight over a long run. Every point was right. No geometry changed.

  • Spring: the spec gave only 0.5 × 5 × 25 mm, with no coil count, solid height or allowed deflection. Now a coil-count window, a screening test, and a printed gauge to do it with.
  • A printed safety clip, so that a knock on a paddle cannot open the lock.
  • Two claims toned down: that one bolt alone is enough (in CAD the leg still cannot get out, but the last stop then works up to three times as hard), and that a servo gives way before the dock does (true only for loads in the plane the leg bends in).
  • Corrected: a print note that said no supports, and a simulation log that belonged to an earlier model.

5 Oct · read-only

Review 2 by GPT

Five points, all valid, each recomputed before changing anything.

  • Safety clip: could be fitted backwards, and had 0.06 mm of room. Version 2 has a key tooth, 0.46 mm of room and a direction mark.
  • Spring: the claim that it works became a list of candidates to be screened. Then a catalogue spring with published rate and solid height was chosen: 3.4 N when locked, 37.4 % of its minimum tensile strength with the bolt pushed all the way in.
  • Gauge: one pin with two reading lines instead of two pins read at the stop.
  • Walking loads: recomputed over all 225,316 recorded samples of the six runs instead of a sample of them. One run rose from 33.5 to 49.3 N: a missed peak.
5 to 7 Oct

First printed parts

The PLA parts of one leg and one body face are printed. Springs and bearings have not arrived, so the lock has only been handled without springs.

5 Oct

Printing starts

One leg on two plates in PLA on the Bambu H2D, plus one body face and the spring gauge, to try the quick release before printing the body, the longest print.

Infill 50 % → 30 % with six walls: 345.5 → 322.3 g for the four large parts, 3.5 h less by the PrusaSlicer estimate. With six walls most of each section is wall already.

6 Oct

First assembly: a bolt would not come out

The two bolts are mirror images, and each one fits the other one’s channel. They had gone in swapped. Seen from the side, the paddle in the photo overhangs the bolt tip by about 8 mm towards the apex and is flush on the base side; as designed it is 1.5 mm and 6.4 mm.

  • The gap in the design: nothing made the swap impossible, and the only marks were small dimples on the paddle (one on the right bolt, two on the left). Mirrored parts that fit each other’s place will be swapped.
  • Now: the rule is that the paddle points at the stop wall. Possible fix, drafted in CAD and on hold until the springs are tested: a 45° key in the channel, so that the wrong bolt cannot enter.
  • Also found that day: under load the bolt does not sit in the middle of its channel but 1.9° askew. So it stands 7.7 mm out, not 7.0; the room on the deep side is 0.51 mm, not 0.70; the flat faces meet after 0.95 mm, not 0.70. From now on, fits are checked with each moving part where it rests under load.

6 Oct

Bearing seats, before the bearings arrive

Two 6806-2RS (30 × 42 × 7 mm) sit stacked in the tower. Neither seat is plain: the bore is Ø42.4 with 8 crush ribs to Ø41.7, the shaft Ø29.7 with ribs to Ø30.3. Measured on the STL files that were printed.

The ribs give way when the bearing is pressed in. The bore may print 0.3 mm small or 0.2 mm large and still grip. Not tested: how tight it is on this printer. Probably tight: if a bearing will not press in, scrape the ribs; if it is loose, a drop of CA glue on the ribs. No reprint either way.

One crush rib, enlarged

The wall itself never touches the bearing: the bore is 0.4 mm larger than the ring. Only the 8 ribs do, each 1.2 mm wide, standing 0.35 mm out from the wall and 0.15 mm into the ring.

7 Oct

Safety clip on the real part

The clip is 1.19 g (CAD) and the same part on both sides. It feels loose mainly because the springs are not in yet: by CAD, the bolt can still slide about 3 mm and roll 3 to 4° in its channel. A spring will hold the bolt against the wall; the clip itself keeps its 0.2 mm of play per side. Whether both hooks have clicked in is not checked yet.

7 Oct

Where it stands

What is established, what has been seen on real parts, and what is not known yet.

Checked in CAD and simulation

  • Fit, release path and collisions on all 20 faces
  • Screening loads: the largest static case puts 208 N on one stop with both bolts out (285 N with one knocked in), against 393 N
  • Crawl: 40 cycles in simulation (model 3.31 kg; the lock is not in that model)

Seen on printed parts

  • One leg, one body face, bolts, clips and the spring gauge printed in PLA
  • A swapped pair of bolts jams: a gap in the design; a keyed channel is drafted, on hold
  • On video the safety clip sits in the drawn place, the right way round

Still open

  • The lock with springs in: does the bolt click out to about 7.7 mm, does the leg stop within a millimetre when pulled, 30 times on and off
  • Spring screening in the gauge: 350 g at the locked length (290 to 410)
  • Press fit of the bearings
  • The tilted test face in the body material, before printing the body
  • Stiffness of one HTD-45H on the bench: the simulation assumes it deflects 1.6° under 1 N·m
  • Creep, fatigue and impact are not analysed. Face 9 and the five cap faces are not cleared for load-carrying legs
